# Potential buyers and partners for Revo's SQL datasets

Research checked: 7 October 2026. This is a sourced prospect inventory, not a list of confirmed buyers. It contains **41 prospects and 4 watchlist entries**. **21 of the 41 prospects have a publicly published email address**; other entries have official routing pages. It cannot establish every possible buyer worldwide.

The companion [CSV](sql-dataset-buyers-20261007.csv) includes product evidence, email evidence, contact purpose, recommended offer, target team, priority, limitations and qualification status for every row. No email address was guessed. No email was sent, form submitted, or inbox deliverability tested.

## What was matched to this project

The active file `runs/production-001/quality/export/golden.jsonl` contains **232 records, 214 unique task IDs, all labeled TRAIN**. Its task families are aggregation (23), deduplication (35), funnel (8), multi-joins (50), simple joins (38), retention (45) and subqueries (33). These are record counts, not independent task counts.

The project builds synthetic analytical SQL tasks with schema/fixtures, candidate SQL and verification/quality evidence, currently using DuckDB. The relevant commercial hypotheses are SQL-model training data, execution-based evaluation cases, and custom business-query test suites. The dataset is not a customer database containing proprietary business records.

Reviewed local context includes `README.md`, `docs/gtm/target-customer.md`, `docs/gtm/product-one-pager.md`, `docs/gtm/technical-datasheet.md`, `docs/licensing.md`, and current production/audit documentation. Existing GTM claims are hypotheses, not customer-demand evidence. Historical verification metadata does not by itself establish that every current record passes the latest audit. Do not make a blanket commercial-readiness or model-improvement claim from this research.

The export's SHA-256 at inspection was `af738a391f9bed0a367ef424b225a819c339aaa7ffcb5f91f02d3e5c76de7c16`. Counts refer to that file, not every newer production run or template in the repository.

## Who to approach first

Priorities are my judgment based on product fit, current dialect fit, reachable contact routes and the small size of this export. They are not measured purchase probabilities.

- **P1:** Direct SQL tools and analytical agents. Start with Defog, MotherDuck, Wren AI, BlazeSQL, AI2SQL, SQLAI.ai, Chat2DB, Querio, NLSQL, Julius and Deepnote.
- **P2:** BI/database product teams and data-delivery partners. Offer a scoped correctness-evaluation or specialist subcontract pilot.
- **P3:** Major cloud vendors and model/training platforms. Pursue once you can show differentiated coverage, clean rights, repeatable generation and a measured result.
- **HOLD:** Historical or strategic leads lacking current product/contact qualification. Keep them out of a routine outreach sequence.

A product that generates SQL is evidence of relevance, not evidence that the company buys external datasets. Many of these companies build their own data, use third-party models, or have customer-specific evaluation needs.

## Offer that matches the current stage

Lead with a **small SQL semantic-correctness pilot**, scoped to the buyer's engine and business-query failure modes. Present a representative sample, executable schema/fixtures, expected results and a reproducible evaluation procedure. Request routing to the text-to-SQL product lead, ML engineering lead or evaluation owner.

For training buyers, describe this export as a small sample of your generation-and-verification capability. Do not claim 232 records are a large training corpus. For evaluation buyers, generate a **new unseen split**: the current file is TRAIN and should not be advertised as a private held-out benchmark. Separate by underlying task/template lineage, not just row IDs.

Before making a customer-facing quality claim, re-run the applicable release/audit checks and document unresolved contract issues. For warehouse products, validate the SQL port and expected-result semantics on the target engine. For semantic BI products, include their metric definitions, joins and modeled context. No performance uplift or willingness to pay has been established.

A suitable positioning sentence, after validating the sample, is: “We create executable analytical SQL tasks with synthetic schemas, fixtures and correctness evidence. We would like to scope an evaluation pilot around your SQL agent's join, deduplication, retention or funnel failure modes.”

## Identity and stale-contact decisions

- Seek AI is counted under IBM, following its [official acquisition page](https://www.seek.ai/ibm). The IBM Labs route is participation/co-creation, not confirmed vendor intake.
- Vanna's [GitHub repository](https://github.com/vanna-ai/vanna) is archived. This does not prove the company has closed; it does justify rechecking the commercial product before outreach.
- MindsDB's homepage now resolves to MindsHub. Its historical support policy does not establish current SQL-data demand.
- Dataherald's partnership email is from a dated official-authored article and is marked historical.
- Anthropic's [sales route](https://claude.com/contact-sales) and [Development Partner Program](https://support.anthropic.com/en/articles/11174108-about-the-development-partner-program) do not establish an open dataset-supplier procurement route.
- Press, legal and privacy-only addresses were excluded from the usable outreach list. Form example addresses were not treated as real contacts.
- Ordinary enterprises, universities and consulting firms were not added merely because they use SQL. Qualifying them would require evidence of an actual text-to-SQL product, funded model training or an evaluation need.

## Practical order of work

1. Contact a small batch of the P1 companies with a product-specific question and a validated sample; use partnership/general routes before support fallbacks.
2. Ask whether their immediate need is evaluation, training, dialect coverage or custom business logic. Ask who owns that budget. Record responses separately from this research.
3. Scope one measurable pilot around an actual failure class and their target database; agree on baseline and acceptance criteria before pricing.
4. Approach Scale and Mercor through their dedicated data-partnership forms to ask whether synthetic executable SQL tasks are eligible. Turing, Toloka and Invisible are possible channels as well as competitors.
5. Approach major labs with a stronger differentiated corpus or generation contract, rather than presenting the existing small TRAIN export as a finished private benchmark.

No company here has confirmed interest, budget, eligibility or willingness to purchase. This inventory establishes relevance and public contact evidence only.
